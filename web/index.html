    How to read it · data archive

    Radar measures how strongly the ground sends its pulse back. Water mirrors it away and looks dark; rough ground, tree crowns and broken ice scatter it back and look bright.

    A change in that echo is a change in roughness or moisture. Flooding, harvest or ice movement are interpretations of it, and we label them that way.

    NISAR flies at 747 km and revisits every 12 days with a 24 cm L-band radar, so it sees day and night, through cloud and smoke.
